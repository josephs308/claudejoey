Wrongful Death

St. Louis Wrongful Death Lawyer

Losing someone you love to another person's carelessness is a wound no legal case can heal. But holding the responsible party accountable — and securing your family's financial future — is something we can help carry for you. When you're ready, Tyler will listen, explain your options plainly, and handle the rest with care.

A wrongful death claim lets certain surviving family members recover compensation when a loved one dies because of someone else's negligence or wrongful act. It cannot undo the loss. What it can do is ease the financial weight left behind and hold the at-fault party responsible — the kind of accountability a grieving family deserves but should never have to pursue alone. Thompson Law STL handles these claims with sensitivity, and with the same resolve we bring to every case.

When a wrongful death claim may apply

A wrongful death claim can arise whenever a death is caused by the kind of negligence or wrongful conduct that would have supported an injury claim had the person survived. Common examples include:

  • Car, truck, and motorcycle crashes
  • Medical malpractice
  • Dangerous or defective products
  • Unsafe premises and other preventable hazards

If you aren't sure whether your situation qualifies, that's exactly what a free, no-pressure conversation is for.

Who can bring a wrongful death claim in Missouri?

Missouri's wrongful death statute does not let just anyone sue. Instead, it sets out a class of people who hold the right, generally in order of priority:

  • First, the spouse, the children of the person who died (or the descendants of any deceased child), and the parents
  • If none of those survive, then the siblings of the person who died, or their descendants
  • If no one in those groups is available, a court may appoint a plaintiff to bring the claim on behalf of those entitled to recover

These rules can get complicated quickly, especially in blended or extended families. One of the first things we do is confirm who holds the right to sue in your specific situation so the claim is brought correctly.

What compensation may be available

A wrongful death recovery is meant to address both the family's concrete financial losses and the profound personal loss. Depending on the facts, damages can include:

  • Funeral and burial expenses
  • The medical bills your loved one incurred before death
  • The lost financial support and benefits the family would have received
  • The survivors' loss of companionship, comfort, guidance, and support

Every family is different. The value of a claim depends on the circumstances of the loss and who depended on your loved one. We take the time to understand your family before putting a number on anything.

A civil claim is separate from any criminal case

Many families assume that if the authorities file criminal charges — or decline to — that decides everything. It does not. A wrongful death claim is a separate civil case brought by the family, and it carries a lower burden of proof than a criminal prosecution. That means a wrongful death claim can move forward whether or not anyone is ever criminally charged, and regardless of how a criminal case ends.

Missouri's deadline for wrongful death

Missouri generally allows three years from the date of death to file a wrongful death claim. We know that thinking about legal deadlines in the middle of grief feels impossible — but the law does impose one, and missing it can close the door for good.

Please don't let the deadline slip. Exceptions and shorter timelines can apply depending on the facts, and they must be confirmed with the firm. There's no pressure to decide anything today; a short call now simply protects your family's options for later.
