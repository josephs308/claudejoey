Car Accidents

St. Louis Car Accident Lawyer

Hurt in a crash that wasn't your fault? You deal with the pain and the bills — we'll deal with the insurance company. And because Tyler Thompson spent years on the insurer's side, he knows exactly how they try to pay you less.

If you were injured in a car accident in Missouri, you generally have the right to recover compensation for your medical bills, lost wages, vehicle damage, and pain and suffering from the at-fault driver. The challenge is that the other driver's insurance company will do everything it can to pay you as little as possible — and Thompson Law STL exists to stop exactly that.

What to do after a car accident

The steps you take in the first hours and days can make or break your claim. If you're able:

  • Call 911 and get checked by a medical professional — even if you feel "fine," adrenaline hides injuries.
  • Photograph everything: vehicle positions, damage, skid marks, the scene, and your injuries.
  • Exchange insurance and contact information and collect the names of any witnesses.
  • Do not admit fault, and do not give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer.
  • Save everything — bills, photos, a crash report — and call a lawyer before accepting any offer.

Watch out: an adjuster may call within days sounding friendly and offering a quick check. That early offer is almost always a fraction of what your claim is worth — and cashing it can end your case for good.

How Thompson Law STL builds your case

We handle the entire claim so you can focus on recovering. That means investigating the crash, preserving evidence, gathering your medical records and wage loss, working with your doctors to document the full extent of your injuries, and putting together a demand the insurer can't dismiss.

Here's the difference: Tyler spent eight years defending insurance companies and four years in-house at a national carrier. He knows how adjusters are trained to value — and devalue — claims. We use that insider knowledge to anticipate their tactics and build a file that's ready for trial from day one. Insurers settle for more when they know your lawyer is genuinely prepared to try the case.

What your claim may include

  • Past and future medical expenses, including therapy and rehabilitation
  • Lost wages and lost earning capacity
  • Property damage to your vehicle
  • Pain, suffering, and loss of enjoyment of life
  • In the most serious cases, long-term care and disability costs

What if the other driver had no insurance?

You may still be covered. Your own uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage can step in — and we regularly pursue these claims, including pushing back when your own insurer tries to lowball you.
