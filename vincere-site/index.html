We show you the math
before you commit.

Most agencies open with a pitch. We open with your numbers: what a customer is worth to you, what your close rate actually is, and what a lead can cost before the channel stops making sense.

If the math doesn’t work in your market, we’ll say so on that call.

01
What a customer is worth. Average value and close rate, in your business, not an industry benchmark. Everything downstream depends on getting this honest.
02
What the market charges. Real cost per click and cost per lead in your geography, pulled before you spend anything.
03
Which channel fits. Some markets are cheap on LSA and brutal on paid search. We’ll tell you which one to start with and why.
04
What our fee looks like. Against those numbers, in writing, before there’s a contract to sign.

Close rate moves more than budget. Lifting lead-to-customer conversion from 15% to 25% does more for cost per customer than adding 40% to spend. That’s why we fix intake and measurement before we launch anything.

Cost per lead is a ceiling, not a goal. A $40 lead that never closes is more expensive than a $200 lead that does. Every channel gets judged on the number at the end of the chain, not the one at the front.

Four steps,
in this order, every time.

The sequence is the whole point. Most agencies start at step three, which is why the reporting never quite reconciles with what showed up in the bank.

01
Audit the market, not just the account. Search volume, competitor spend and real cost per lead in your geography. If the numbers don’t work, we say so.
02
Fix the measurement. Call tracking and conversion events wired to real outcomes. Optimizing toward a broken conversion is the most expensive mistake in this industry.
03
Launch what fits. Usually one or two channels. We’d rather prove one and expand than spread a budget across seven and learn nothing.
04
Report to cost per customer. What each channel produced, what it cost, what changes next, in language you can take straight into a partner meeting.
