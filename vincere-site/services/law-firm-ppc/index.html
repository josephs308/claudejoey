Quick answer

What is PPC for law firms?

PPC for law firms is paid search advertising, mainly Google Search Ads, where your firm bids to appear at the top of results for queries like ’car accident lawyer near me’. You pay per click. Done well, it targets high-intent searches, filters out bad-fit traffic, tracks every call and form, and is judged by cost per signed case.

Why law firm PPC fails for most firms

Legal keywords are among the most expensive in paid search, and the auction rewards whoever is most disciplined, not whoever spends most. Most wasted law firm PPC spend comes from broad match keywords that pull in job seekers, people wanting free legal advice, and searches for the wrong practice area. Add a generic landing page and an intake line that rings out after hours, and the account looks busy while signed cases stay flat.

The fix is not a bigger budget. It is tighter structure: campaigns split by practice area and case type, match types that keep spend on real intent, and a negative keyword list that grows every week from actual search terms. Ad schedules and geographic targeting follow where and when your intake team can actually answer, so you are not paying for calls that go to voicemail.

How we build Google Ads for lawyers

Before any money is spent, the free plan prices your market: typical cost per click for your practice terms, how many firms are bidding, and what a lead is likely to cost. If PPC cannot produce signed cases at a cost your case values support, we tell you and point the budget somewhere better, such as Local Services Ads or SEO.

When the numbers work, we build the account around case intent. Ad copy speaks to the specific problem, like a denied claim or a recent arrest, and is written with state bar advertising rules such as ABA Model Rule 7.1 in mind. Each ad group points to a landing page that matches the search, with click-to-call, a short form and tracked phone numbers.

Tying PPC spend to signed cases

Google Ads will happily report conversions that are really misdials, spam forms and existing clients calling about their file. We connect call tracking and your intake records so the account learns from qualified consultations and signed cases instead. That lets smart bidding optimize toward the leads your firm actually wants, rather than the cheapest form fill. It also shows which case types are worth bidding up.

Every dollar of PPC spend shows up in your client dashboard next to calls, consultations and cost per signed case, alongside your other channels. You can see which campaigns and keywords produce clients, which ones only produce clicks, and where the next dollar should go. Monthly reviews cover search term cleanup, bid strategy, landing page tests and how intake handled the leads.

PPC questions, answered.

PPC is worth it for a law firm when the value of a signed case comfortably covers the cost of acquiring it, and when intake answers fast. In many practice areas and metros that math works well. In others, click costs are too high relative to case value, and Local Services Ads or SEO are better first moves. The free plan prices this before you spend.
A law firm’s Google Ads budget should be set from local click costs, expected conversion rates and how many cases you want each month, not a round number. Too small a budget in a competitive market can produce too few clicks to learn anything. Vincere builds that estimate in the free plan so you see the expected cost per case first.
PPC for lawyers means Google Search Ads, where you bid on keywords and pay per click. Local Services Ads appear above them, require Google Screened verification, and charge per lead rather than per click. Many firms run both, with LSA capturing local intent and PPC covering specific case types and searches LSA does not show for.
Law firm PPC starts producing leads as soon as ads go live, which is why firms use it when they need cases soon. The first weeks are about cutting wasted search terms and learning which campaigns produce consultations. Cost per signed case usually becomes reliable once enough leads have moved through your intake and case evaluation process.
Junk leads from Google Ads usually come from broad keyword matching, weak negative keyword lists and conversion tracking that counts every call as a win. Fixing them means reviewing actual search terms, tightening match types and feeding real intake outcomes back into the account. Landing pages that state what cases you take also filter bad fits.
