Before you talk to the insurer

The adjuster may call before you've left the hospital.

Insurance companies move fast after a crash. An adjuster may ask for a recorded statement or offer a quick check before you know how badly you're hurt. Anything you say can be used to lower your claim, and cashing an early check can end it.

Tyler spent twelve years on the other side of those calls. Talk to him first. It's free, and it may be the most valuable call you make.

Common questions

General information about Missouri and Illinois law, not legal advice for your situation. Call for a free consultation about your case.

How much does a personal injury lawyer cost in St. Louis?

Nothing up front. Thompson Law STL works on a contingency fee, so there is no cost to start and no fee unless we recover money for you. The consultation is free.

How long do I have to file an injury claim in Missouri?

Most Missouri personal injury claims must be filed within five years of the injury. Medical malpractice claims generally have two years and wrongful death claims three. Illinois is generally two years. Exceptions exist, so confirm your deadline early.

Should I talk to the other driver's insurance company?

Be careful. Adjusters are trained to keep payouts low, and a recorded statement can be used against you. Talk to a lawyer before you give a statement or accept an early offer.

What is my case worth?

It depends on your medical bills, lost income, how serious and lasting your injuries are, pain and suffering, and the insurance available. Anyone who quotes a number before reviewing the facts is guessing. A free case review will give you an honest assessment.

Will my case go to court?

Most injury cases settle without a trial. Insurers tend to pay more when they know your lawyer is ready to try the case, so every case is prepared as if it will.

Do you handle cases in Illinois?

Yes. Tyler is licensed in Missouri and Illinois and represents clients across the St. Louis region and Southern Illinois.
