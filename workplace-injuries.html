If someone other than your employer caused your on-the-job injury, you may be able to bring a separate "third-party" personal injury claim for the full range of damages — including pain and suffering — that workers' compensation does not pay. For many injured workers, that third-party claim is the difference between a limited comp check and a recovery that actually reflects what happened to them. Thompson Law STL exists to find it.

Workers' comp is usually just part of the picture

In most cases, workers' compensation is the exclusive remedy against your employer. It's a trade-off: you generally can't sue your employer, but you also don't have to prove anyone was at fault to receive benefits. The catch is that comp is limited. It typically covers your medical treatment and a portion of your lost wages — but it does not pay for pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, or the full value of your future losses.

Workers' comp vs. a third-party injury claim

These are two different claims, and understanding the difference is where real money is often left on the table:

  • Workers' compensation — a no-fault claim against your employer. No need to prove blame, but limited benefits and no pain and suffering.
  • Third-party personal injury claim — a claim against someone other than your employer whose negligence hurt you. You must prove fault, but you can recover the full range of damages comp leaves out.

Who might be a liable third party?

Work injuries often involve people and companies beyond your employer. Depending on how you were hurt, a third party might be:

  • A negligent subcontractor or another contractor's crew on a shared job site
  • A careless driver who hit you while you were working or driving for the job
  • A property owner who allowed a dangerous condition where you were sent to work
  • The manufacturer of a defective machine, tool, or product that failed and injured you

These claims are especially common on construction sites and in delivery and driving jobs, where many different companies and vehicles share the same space.

Good to know: in many cases you can pursue workers' compensation and a third-party injury claim at the same time. Done right, the two work together — and we coordinate them so your comp benefits don't quietly eat into your recovery.

What a third-party claim can recover

Unlike comp, a third-party claim aims at the full value of your losses:

  • Past and future medical expenses
  • Full lost wages and lost earning capacity
  • Pain, suffering, and loss of enjoyment of life
  • Long-term care and disability costs in the most serious cases

Workplace Injuries: common questions

General information about Missouri and Illinois law, not legal advice for your situation. Deadlines and rules vary, so call for a free consultation about your case.

Can I sue my employer for a work injury?

Generally no — in most cases workers' comp is the exclusive remedy against your employer, with only narrow exceptions. But you can usually pursue a separate personal injury claim against a negligent third party whose carelessness caused your injury.

What's the difference between workers' comp and a personal injury claim?

Comp is no-fault but limited — it covers medical care and part of your lost wages and generally pays no pain and suffering. A third-party injury claim requires proving someone else was at fault, but it can recover the full range of damages comp leaves out.

Can I get workers' comp and sue a third party too?

Often, yes. In many cases you can collect workers' compensation and also bring a separate third-party claim against the person or company whose negligence caused the injury. We coordinate the two so they work in your favor.
