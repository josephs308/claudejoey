Slip & Fall

St. Louis Slip and Fall Lawyer

Fell because of a hazard someone else left unfixed? Property owners and businesses have a legal duty to keep their premises reasonably safe — and when they ignore it, you shouldn't be left with the bills. Because Tyler Thompson spent years on the insurer's side, he knows exactly how these claims are defended.

If you were hurt because a property owner or business let a dangerous condition go unaddressed, you generally have the right to recover compensation for your medical bills, lost wages, and pain and suffering. These cases — known as premises liability claims — are rarely as simple as insurers make them sound, and Thompson Law STL exists to prove exactly what happened and who was responsible.

What is premises liability?

In Missouri, property owners and businesses generally owe their lawful visitors a duty to keep the premises reasonably safe, and to fix — or at least warn about — hazards they know about or should have discovered through reasonable care. When that duty is ignored and someone gets hurt, the owner can be held responsible. Common dangerous conditions include:

  • Wet or freshly mopped floors with no warning sign
  • Spills and debris left unattended in aisles and entryways
  • Ice and snow that wasn't cleared or treated
  • Uneven, cracked, or broken walkways, steps, and parking lots
  • Poor or burned-out lighting in stairwells and garages
  • Loose rugs, mats, and torn carpeting
  • Missing or broken handrails

What you have to prove

Falling on someone's property is not, by itself, enough to win a case. In most cases you have to show four things: that the owner owed you a duty, that a dangerous condition existed, that the owner knew or should have known about it and failed to fix it or warn you, and that the condition actually caused your injury. That middle piece — legal "notice" — is where most slip and fall claims are won or lost, and it's where an experienced attorney makes the difference.

Watch out: the evidence that proves notice — incident reports, maintenance logs, and especially surveillance video — can be overwritten or erased within days. The sooner we send a preservation demand, the better your odds of proving what the owner knew.

Is the store automatically responsible?

No. A business is not liable simply because a customer fell on its premises. Insurers and defense lawyers lean on this hard, arguing the hazard was "open and obvious" or that there was no way the owner could have known. We counter by digging for the proof of negligence — how long the spill sat there, whether staff followed their own inspection schedule, and what the cameras actually show.

What if I was partly at fault?

Missouri follows a comparative fault rule. If you're found partly responsible — say, for not watching where you were walking — your recovery is generally reduced by your percentage of fault rather than wiped out entirely. Insurers routinely exaggerate a victim's share of the blame to shrink the payout, and part of our job is to keep that number honest.

Common slip and fall injuries

A fall that sounds minor can cause lasting harm, particularly for older adults. We frequently represent clients with:

  • Broken bones and fractures, especially wrists and ankles
  • Hip injuries, which can require surgery and long recoveries
  • Head and traumatic brain injuries
  • Back and spine injuries, including herniated discs
