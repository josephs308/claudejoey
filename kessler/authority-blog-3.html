How Much Is My Personal Injury Case Worth in New York?

✎ Daniel Kessler, Esq. 📅 April 18, 2025 🕐 8 min read

Herniated discs requiring surgery: $150,000–$500,000+. Traumatic brain injuries: $500,000–$3,000,000+. Spinal cord injuries: $2,000,000–$20,000,000+. Pain and suffering — which has no cap in New York personal injury cases — is typically worth 1.5 to 5 times your total medical bills. The first insurance offer is almost never the right number. — Daniel Kessler, Esq., Kessler & Associates, New York

The Question Everyone Has — And Almost No One Answers Honestly

"How much is my case worth?" is the first thing most injured people want to know. It's also the question most attorneys dodge with some version of "it depends." That's not good enough. Here's a real answer — how personal injury damages are actually calculated in New York, what factors move the number up or down, and why the first offer from the insurance company is almost never the right one.

Two Categories of Damages: Economic and Non-Economic

Every personal injury case in New York involves two types of recoverable damages.

Economic damages are the financial losses you can document: medical bills, future medical costs, lost wages, reduced earning capacity, property damage. These are calculated from records — bills, pay stubs, tax returns, expert projections.

Non-economic damages are the human costs that don't come with a receipt: pain and suffering, emotional distress, loss of enjoyment of life, loss of consortium. These are real, legally recognized damages — and they're often worth more than the economic damages in serious cases.

New York has no cap on non-economic damages in personal injury cases (unlike medical malpractice, which has different rules). This is one of the reasons New York produces some of the largest personal injury verdicts in the country.

How Pain and Suffering Is Calculated

There's no formula that produces a precise number, but there are two methods commonly used.

The multiplier method takes your total economic damages and multiplies by a factor typically between 1.5 and 5 — depending on the severity and permanence of the injury, the impact on your life, and the quality of the evidence. A $100,000 medical bill case might yield $200,000 to $500,000 in pain and suffering using this method.

The per diem method assigns a daily dollar value to your suffering and multiplies by the number of days you've suffered. If a jury believes your pain is worth $150 per day and you'll suffer for 20 more years (7,300 days), that's $1.095 million in pain and suffering alone.

Experienced plaintiff's attorneys present both calculations to juries and let them choose the more favorable result. Insurance companies know this — which is why they try to settle before trial.

What Makes a Case Worth More

Several factors consistently push case values higher:

Permanent injuries. An injury that heals completely in six months is worth significantly less than one that permanently limits your function. Spinal cord damage, traumatic brain injury, loss of a limb, permanent scarring — these injuries carry lifetime consequences that juries take seriously.

Clear liability. When the at-fault party's negligence is undeniable — a drunk driver, a safety violation with documented evidence, a prior complaint about the same hazard — defendants settle higher because they fear what a jury will do.

Significant impact on daily life. Documented evidence that your injury affected your work, your relationships, your ability to care for your children, your hobbies — this evidence is gold at trial and in negotiations.

Young plaintiffs. A 35-year-old with a permanent injury has more years of suffering ahead of them than a 65-year-old. Age affects both the economic damages calculation (more years of lost earning capacity) and the non-economic calculation.

Egregious conduct. When defendants act recklessly or maliciously — drunk driving, falsified truck logbooks, a property owner who ignored repeated complaints — punitive damages may be available on top of compensatory damages.

What Makes a Case Worth Less

Insurance companies and defense attorneys look for specific factors to reduce case value:

Gaps in medical treatment. If you stopped treating for six weeks and then resumed, defense attorneys will argue your injuries weren't that serious or that something else caused your current condition. Consistent treatment is not just medically important — it's legally important.

Pre-existing conditions. Prior injuries to the same body part create ammunition for the defense. This doesn't eliminate your case — you're entitled to recover for the aggravation of a pre-existing condition — but it requires careful medical documentation to distinguish what was pre-existing from what the accident caused.

Comparative fault. Under New York's pure comparative negligence system, your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault. If you were 20% at fault and your damages are $500,000, you recover $400,000. Defense attorneys routinely try to inflate your fault percentage — that's exactly what our attorneys counter with evidence.

Policy limits. A case can be worth $2 million but only recover $100,000 if the at-fault driver carries minimum insurance and has no significant assets. This is why examining all available coverage — umbrella policies, employer policies, underinsured motorist coverage — is critical before settling.

Why the First Offer Is Almost Never Right

Insurance companies make early settlement offers for a reason: they work. Studies show that unrepresented claimants settle for significantly less than represented ones — even after attorney fees.

Early offers are made before the full extent of your injuries is known, before your future medical costs are projected, before your lost earning capacity is calculated, and before your case is fully documented. You simply cannot know what your case is worth in the first weeks after an accident. Signing a release at that stage permanently closes the case — forever.

The adjuster who calls you is trained to be sympathetic, to make the offer seem reasonable, and to create urgency. The offer has an expiration date by design. Don't fall for it.

Real New York Case Values: A Range

These are general ranges based on case type — actual values depend on the specific facts:

  • Minor soft tissue injury (full recovery): $15,000 – $75,000
  • Herniated disc requiring surgery: $150,000 – $500,000+
  • Fractured bones with complications: $100,000 – $400,000
  • Traumatic brain injury (moderate): $500,000 – $3,000,000+
  • Spinal cord injury / paralysis: $2,000,000 – $20,000,000+
  • Wrongful death: $1,000,000 – $20,000,000+

These numbers are not guarantees. But they illustrate why a $45,000 offer two weeks after a serious accident is almost never the right number.

How to Protect the Value of Your Case

  • Treat consistently — no gaps
  • Document everything your injury prevents you from doing
  • Don't post on social media
  • Don't give recorded statements to the other driver's insurer
  • Don't sign anything without an attorney reviewing it first
  • Hire an attorney who prepares every case for trial — settlements go higher when the other side knows you'll actually try the case

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a cap on pain and suffering damages in New York?

No. New York does not cap non-economic damages in personal injury cases. Pain and suffering, emotional distress, and loss of enjoyment of life are unlimited. This is why New York produces some of the largest personal injury verdicts in the country — and why insurers work so hard to settle before trial.

How is pain and suffering actually calculated in New York?

Two methods are used: the multiplier method (multiply total medical bills by 1.5–5 based on severity) and the per diem method (assign a daily dollar value × number of days suffering). At Kessler & Associates, we present both calculations and use whichever is more favorable. Insurers know this — it’s why they prefer to settle before we put the numbers in front of a jury.

How long does a personal injury case take to settle in New York?

Clear-liability cases with documented injuries often settle in 6–12 months. Complex or disputed cases typically take 1–3 years. Settling too quickly — before the full extent of your injuries is established — can permanently undervalue your case by hundreds of thousands of dollars.

What is a personal injury case actually worth in New York City?

NYC juries award higher verdicts than most US jurisdictions. General ranges: minor soft tissue injuries $15K–$75K, herniated disc surgery $150K–$500K+, TBI $500K–$3M+, spinal cord injury $2M–$20M+. These are ranges, not guarantees. The free case evaluation at Kessler & Associates gives you a specific, honest assessment of your case.
