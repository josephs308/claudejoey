Common questions

What New York clients ask first.

New York is a no-fault state with $50,000 in mandatory Personal Injury Protection. Your own insurer pays your medical bills and 80% of lost wages regardless of who caused the crash. But if your injury meets the serious injury definition in Insurance Law § 5102(d) — a fracture, significant disfigurement, permanent limitation, or inability to perform your normal activities for 90 of the first 180 days — you step outside no-fault and sue for everything, pain and suffering included.

Three years for most personal injury claims. Two years and six months for medical malpractice. Two years for wrongful death. And 90 days to serve a Notice of Claim on New York City, the MTA, or any municipality — a deadline that catches more valid cases than any other rule in this state.

The Scaffold Law. It imposes absolute liability on property owners and general contractors when a worker is hurt in a fall or by a falling object. You do not have to prove anyone was careless — only that the gravity-related accident happened. It is the strongest construction-worker protection in the country, and it cannot be waived.

Yes. New York applies pure comparative negligence, so you can recover even if you were 99% responsible; your award is reduced by your share and never eliminated. Adjusters inflate your percentage because it is the cheapest lever they have. Evidence is how you push it back.
