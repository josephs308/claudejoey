Why this case matters

What actually decides the value of this claim.

Premises liability covers injuries caused by unsafe conditions on someone else’s property — slip and falls, elevator and escalator failures, staircase and ceiling collapses, inadequate security. In a city of aging buildings these claims are constant, and they turn on one question: did the owner know about the hazard, or should they have known with reasonable inspection.

We move immediately on these files because the evidence has a shelf life measured in weeks. Surveillance is overwritten. The condition gets repaired. The incident report goes into a drawer. Preservation letters go out the day you retain us.

What you need to know

Read these six things before you talk to an adjuster.

Each one has changed the value of a real file. Most claimants learn them too late to use.

The central issueActual versus constructive notice

An owner is liable if they knew of the hazard, or if it existed long enough that reasonable inspection would have found it. Prior complaints, work orders and earlier incidents at the same spot are how notice gets proved.

NYC Admin. Code § 7-210In New York City, sidewalks belong to the owner

The abutting property owner, not the City, is responsible for maintaining the sidewalk. A fall on a broken flag outside a building is generally a claim against that building.

Gen. Mun. Law § 50-eGovernment property: 90 days

A claim against the City, NYCHA, the MTA or a public authority requires a Notice of Claim within 90 days of the incident. It is a hard deadline with almost no exceptions.

Spoliation lettersFootage disappears in 30 to 90 days

Most commercial systems overwrite on a fixed cycle. A preservation letter creates a duty to retain — and destruction after that letter is itself sanctionable.

ForeseeabilityInadequate security is a premises claim

Owners owe a duty to take minimal precautions against foreseeable criminal harm. Where an assault occurs in a building with broken locks and a documented history, the owner may be liable.

DOB violations · maintenance logsElevators leave a paper trail

New York elevator and escalator owners carry strict maintenance and inspection obligations. The logs, the service contract and the Department of Buildings violation history are the case.

How we handle it

From your first call, in order.

Five stages. The sequence matters — evidence secured late is evidence lost.

Immediate preservation

Legal preservation letters go out within hours — surveillance footage, incident reports, maintenance and inspection records, and any prior complaints about the same condition.

Scene investigation

We document conditions as they were: lighting levels, surface friction, signage, measurements and photographs, before anything is repaired or replaced.

Notice investigation

We pull prior incidents, 311 and tenant complaints, work orders and Department of Buildings violations to establish that the owner knew or should have known.

Medical documentation

We coordinate with your treating physicians to document every injury and its long-term consequences, and to keep the record free of the gaps the defense needs.

Demand and resolution

We present the claim supported by evidence and expert opinion. Where owners and their carriers will not pay fairly, we try it.
