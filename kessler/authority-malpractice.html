WHY THIS CASE MATTERS

Medical malpractice occurs when a healthcare provider's conduct falls below the accepted standard of care and causes harm. These are the most complex personal injury cases — the defendants are well-funded, the evidence is technical, and the legal requirements are demanding. WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Before you talk to the insurance company, read these facts. They can be worth everything.

Standard of Care Is the Legal Test

Malpractice requires proving the provider's conduct fell below what a reasonably competent provider in the same specialty would have done. Expert testimony is required.

No Cap on Damages in New York

Unlike many states, New York has no cap on non-economic damages in medical malpractice cases. Pain and suffering, permanent disability, and loss of enjoyment of life are fully recoverable.

Statute of Limitations: 2.5 Years

New York gives you 2 years and 6 months from the date of malpractice — or from the end of continuous treatment — to file. For minors, the clock doesn't start until age 18. Government hospital claims: 90-day Notice of Claim.

Hospitals Bear Direct Liability

Not just individual doctors. Hospitals can be liable for inadequate staffing, supervision failures, and systemic policy violations.

Informed Consent Is Separate

If a doctor performed a procedure without adequately explaining the risks and you would have refused with proper information, that's a separate, actionable claim.

Birth Injuries Have Extended Deadlines

For birth injury cases, the child has until their 10th birthday to file in New York — giving families time to understand the full extent of permanent injuries.
