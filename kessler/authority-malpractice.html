Why this case matters

What actually decides the value of this claim.

Malpractice is not a bad outcome. It is care that fell below the accepted standard and caused harm — two separate things, each of which must be proved by a qualified expert in the same specialty. These are the most technical and most expensively defended cases in civil litigation, and they are lost far more often on preparation than on facts.

Sarah Chen brings Columbia Law training and years inside hospital risk management. She understands how these institutions document, review and protect themselves, because helping them do it was once her job.

What you need to know

Read these six things before you talk to an adjuster.

Each one has changed the value of a real file. Most claimants learn them too late to use.

Expert testimony requiredStandard of care is the legal test

You must show the provider’s conduct fell below what a reasonably competent provider in the same specialty would have done, and that the departure caused your injury. Both elements need a credentialed expert.

No limit on non-economic damagesNew York caps nothing

Unlike most states, New York places no cap on pain and suffering, permanent disability or loss of enjoyment of life in a malpractice case. It is one reason New York verdicts are among the largest in the country.

CPLR § 214-aTwo years and six months

Measured from the act of malpractice, or from the end of a continuous course of treatment for the same condition. A public hospital requires a Notice of Claim within 90 days.

Not only the individual doctorHospitals bear their own liability

Inadequate staffing, failed supervision, credentialing failures and systemic policy breaches are direct institutional liability — and the institution carries far more insurance than the physician.

Pub. Health Law § 2805-dInformed consent is a separate claim

If the risks were never adequately explained and a reasonable patient would have declined the procedure with that information, that is an independent, actionable claim even where the procedure itself was performed competently.

Until the child’s 10th birthdayBirth injury deadlines run longer

Infancy tolls the statute, giving families time to understand the full extent of a permanent injury before the claim must be filed. Waiting still costs evidence.

How we handle it

From your first call, in order.

Five stages. The sequence matters — evidence secured late is evidence lost.

Medical expert review

Sarah Chen and our expert network review your complete records to assess whether the standard of care was breached and whether that breach caused the harm. There is no cost to you for this review.

Evidence preservation

Records, imaging, surgical notes, nursing flow sheets, incident reports and internal protocols are demanded immediately. Knowing what exists is half the work.

Expert network assembly

We retain board-certified specialists in the relevant field who will sign the certificate of merit and testify to the departure and its consequences.

Economic damages analysis

Economists and certified life-care planners document future medical costs, lost earning capacity and the full lifetime financial impact in present-value terms.

Trial preparation

Malpractice defendants rarely pay well before trial is credible. We build for the courtroom and negotiate from there.
