WHY THIS CASE MATTERS

New York has the strongest construction worker protection laws in the country. Labor Law 240 — the Scaffold Law — imposes absolute liability on property owners and general contractors when a worker is injured in a fall or by a falling object. WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Before you talk to the insurance company, read these facts. They can be worth everything.

Labor Law 240 — Absolute Liability

New York's Scaffold Law imposes absolute liability on owners and contractors for gravity-related accidents — falls from height, falling objects, scaffold collapses. This is the most plaintiff-friendly construction law in the country.

Labor Law 241 — Safety Violations

Section 241(6) creates liability when specific Industrial Code violations contribute to an injury. We identify every applicable violation in your case.

Multiple Defendants

Construction accidents typically involve multiple liable parties — general contractors, subcontractors, property owners, equipment manufacturers. We pursue all of them simultaneously.

OSHA Violations Are Powerful

Federal and state OSHA violations create strong evidence of negligence. Marcus Webb's OSHA background means we find violations others miss.

Workers Comp Is Just the Beginning

Workers comp covers medical bills and partial wages but not pain and suffering. When a third party contributed — contractor, equipment maker, property owner — we pursue a separate personal injury claim that can be worth significantly more.

Undocumented Workers Have Full Rights

Immigration status does not affect your right to compensation for construction injuries in New York. Period.

COMMON QUESTIONS

New York Labor Law 240 imposes absolute liability on property owners and general contractors for gravity-related accidents on construction sites. It's one of the most powerful worker protection statutes in the country.

Workers comp is generally the exclusive remedy against your direct employer. But if a third party — another contractor, property owner, equipment manufacturer — contributed to your injury, you can sue them for full damages including pain and suffering.

Your immigration status has no bearing on your right to compensation under New York law. We handle cases for injured workers regardless of status.

Waivers of Labor Law 240 and 241 rights are generally unenforceable in New York. You cannot waive these statutory protections.
